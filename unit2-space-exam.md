## Answers with explanations


### Section A — Vocabulary: Word Meanings (1–20)

**1. B — space**  
*Space* is defined as everything above (beyond) the Earth.

**2. C — satellite**  
A *satellite* is an object sent into space to collect information.

**3. B — the Moon**  
*Lunar* means "of the Moon", so a *lunar capsule* is a Moon capsule.

**4. D — astronaut**  
An *astronaut* is a person who travels to and explores space.

**5. A — to send something up into the air**  
*Launch* = to send something into the air, as with a space rocket.

**6. B — to bring something down to the ground**  
*Land* = to bring something down onto a surface, e.g. "The lunar capsule lands on the Moon."

**7. A — capsule**  
A *capsule* is a small container where astronauts live and travel.

**8. A — the activity of going somewhere never visited by humans before**  
*Exploration* is the activity of travelling to a place that has never been visited.

**9. B — 2.54 centimetres**  
An *inch* is 2.54 centimetres.

**10. C — clip**  
A *clip* is a short audio or video file.

**11. A — to look for information**  
*Research* is looking carefully for information.

**12. B — thunderstorm**  
A *thunderstorm* is bad weather with rain, thunder and lightning.

**13. D — disc**  
A *disc* is a round, flat item like a CD, DVD or record.

**14. B — made of gold, or gold-coloured**  
*Golden* = made of gold or gold in colour, as in the *Golden Record*.

**15. C — scientist**  
A *scientist* is a person who works in science.

**16. B — to think or dream about something**  
*To imagine* is to think or dream about something that is not real.

**17. A — science fiction**  
*Science fiction* is a type of book or movie about science, space and the future.

**18. C — happy because you, or someone you know, did something good**  
*To be proud* = to feel happy because you or someone you know did something good.

**19. B — land**  
*Launch* sends something up into the air; *land* brings it back down. They are opposite actions.

**20. D — launch**  
*Launch* is a verb (an action). *Exploration* is a noun; *lunar* and *golden* are adjectives.


### Section B — Vocabulary: Compound Nouns and Word Building (21–32)

**21. A — two words put together to make a new word**  
A compound noun is made of two words joined together to make one new word, e.g. *launch + pad = launch pad*.

**22. B — launch pad**  
*Launch* (to send into the air) + *pad* (a flat surface) = *launch pad*.

**23. A — to land a capsule on the Moon**  
*Lunar* (of the Moon) + *landing* = landing a capsule on the Moon.

**24. C — air supply**  
*Air supply* is the air that astronauts breathe inside the capsule.

**25. B — sunlight**  
*Sun* + *light* = *sunlight*, the light from the sun.

**26. A — to fly through space**  
*Space* + *flight* = flying through space.

**27. C — to move up**  
*Up* + *ward* = moving in an upward direction.

**28. C — space station**  
A *space station*, such as the International Space Station, is where astronauts live and do research.

**29. A — zero gravity**  
*Zero gravity* means there is no force pulling you towards the Earth, so astronauts float.

**30. D — artificial light**  
*Artificial light* is light made by people, for example from a lamp or a battery.

**31. C — astronaut**  
*Astronaut* is a single word, not two words joined together. The others are all made of two words.

**32. B — engine**  
*Rocket* + *engine* = *rocket engine*, the part that gives the rocket its power.

### Section D — Grammar: The Second Conditional (45–62)

**45. B — something we imagine, which is not real now**  
The second conditional talks about something we imagine in the present or the future, but which is not real.

**46. C — If + past simple, would + bare infinitive**  
The pattern is: **If + past simple, would + bare infinitive**, e.g. *If I went to space, I would take a camera.*

**47. D — went**  
After *if* in the second conditional we use the past simple: *went*.

**48. B — would travel**  
The result clause uses *would* + bare infinitive: *would travel*.

**49. B — were**  
In the second conditional we use the past form of *be*, and *were* is used for all persons: *If I were an astronaut …*

**50. C — lived**  
The *if* clause takes the past simple, even when it comes second in the sentence: *if she lived*.

**51. C — If I had a rocket, I would fly to the Moon.**  
Only C follows the rule *If + past simple, would + bare infinitive*. We never use *would* directly after *if* in this structure.

**52. A — wouldn't**  
A negative second conditional result uses *wouldn't* + bare infinitive.

**53. B — didn't have**  
The *if* clause needs a negative past simple: *didn't have*.

**54. D — Would**  
To make a second conditional question we start with *Would* + subject + bare infinitive.

**55. B — would**  
After the question word we use *would*: *Where would you go …?*

**56. C — I would**  
In the second conditional, *'d* is the short form of *would*.

**57. C — would take**  
The result clause of a second conditional uses *would* + bare infinitive: *would take*.

**58. D — had**  
The *if* clause uses the past simple: *had*.

**59. A — (1) is the first conditional and (2) is the second conditional.**  
Sentence (1) uses *present simple + will* (first conditional, a real possibility). Sentence (2) uses *past simple + would* (second conditional, imagined).

**60. B — If I had a spaceship, I would visit Mars every weekend.**  
Only B uses the second conditional (*had … would visit*), which shows an imaginary situation.

**61. C — If I went to space, I would grow vegetables.**  
The correct order follows *If + past simple, would + bare infinitive*.

**62. B — would**  
The speaker is imagining life in space, so the second conditional form *would be* is used.


### Section E — Grammar: Noun Phrases and Functional Language (63–70)

**63. B — You're right.**  
*You're right*, *Me too* and *I agree* are used for agreeing.

**64. D — I'm not so sure.**  
*I'm not so sure* and *I don't really agree* are used to disagree politely.

**65. A — describes the second word**  
In a compound noun the first word describes the second, e.g. *space mission* tells you the exact type of mission.

**66. A — give the reader more information about the noun**  
Expanded noun phrases add extra detail so the reader knows more about what is being described.

**67. C — the red planet in space**  
*The red planet in space* is a noun phrase with extra information added (*red*, *in space*).

**68. B — old**  
*Spaceship* is the main noun; *old* is the additional information that describes it.

**69. C — planet**  
*Planet* is the main noun; *tiny* and *rocky* are adjectives that describe it.

**70. A — a beautiful sunset over the red mountains**  
A is a correct, natural expansion: the extra phrase *over the red mountains* adds more detail about the sunset.

### Section I — Extra Practice: The Second Conditional (96–105)

**96. B — knew**  
The *if* clause of a second conditional takes the past simple: *knew*.

**97. B — would**  
The result clause takes *would* + bare infinitive: *would take*.

**98. B — What would you do if you saw an alien?**  
A second conditional question is *question word + would + subject + bare infinitive*, with the past simple after *if*.

**99. A — The speaker has a telescope.**  
The second conditional imagines the opposite of what is real. The imagined situation is *not* having a telescope, so in reality the speaker **does** have one.

**100. B — were**  
This is an imagined situation, so we use the past form *were* after *if*.

**101. B — didn’t get**  
A negative *if* clause in the second conditional uses *didn’t* + bare infinitive.

**102. A — If I had a rocket, I would travel to the Moon.**  
The real situation is negative, so the imagined version turns it positive: *had* … *would travel*.

**103. C — If I go to the museum, I will see a real rocket.**  
Option C uses *present simple + will*, which is the first conditional — a real possibility, not an imagined one.

**104. A — Would**  
Second conditional questions begin with *Would* + subject + bare infinitive.

**105. A — the bare infinitive, e.g. take**  
*Would* is always followed by the bare infinitive — the verb with no *to* and no ending.
