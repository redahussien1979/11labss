C. Notes on Credit Scorecard and score enabled segments

POLICY TEXTPolicy page 5
  1. For all PF segments, Application Score is used, Behavioral scorecard is not applicable
  2. Non-scored segments are defined and signed off with rationale by GCRO, please refer to approved memo "Scorecard Exclusion Segments, November 2022"

  4. Auto Pass: A case is considered as Auto Pass when it satisfies all policy parameters
  5. Straight Pass (STP): A case is considered as STP if system flags it as Auto Pass and CPV flag check is Waived. However, RCD may manually change the Auto Pass or CPV flag using Manual Deviation for exceptional cases to clarify any doubt. An Auto Pass case where CPV is not waived, RCD may waive CPV as per CPV approved policy and change it to STP.
    Depending on Employer Remarks Code in Finnone, scorecard can either reject the deal or reduce eligible multiples as per below grid for High & Medium Risk. No change in scorecard decision for Low and Very Low risk.
IN SIMPLE WORDS
  • PF uses only the Application scorecard (not the behavioural one).
  • Which segments are not scored is fixed by the GCRO memo "Scorecard Exclusion Segments, November 2022".
  • Auto Pass = all policy parameters met. STP (Straight Pass) = Auto Pass and CPV waived.
  • Digital journey auto-pass is capped at AED 175,000 (Expat) and AED 500,000 (UAE National). The conventional (branch) journey has no auto-pass amount ceiling.
  • RCD can manually change the Auto Pass / CPV flag (Manual Deviation) to clear doubts, and can waive CPV on an Auto Pass case (as per CPV policy) to make it STP.
WORKED EXAMPLE – DIGITAL CEILING
Customer L (Expat) applies on the digital journey for AED 200,000 and meets every policy parameter.
200,000 > 175,000 (Expat digital auto-pass ceiling)
→ Cannot be auto-passed on the digital journey. The same request from a UAE National (ceiling 500,000) could auto-pass. Through the conventional journey there is no auto-pass amount ceiling.

D. AECB Policy Checks (score enabled segments)

POLICY TEXTPolicy page 5
Below are the automated checks for the score enabled segments
  1. Auto Reject if:
    • If AECB score is 300 for Expat and Local for all segments
    • Expats and UAE Nationals if score is < 541 and segment is PDC
    • If overdue amount is > AED 10,000 for Expats for all segments
  2. Current overdue of > AED 1000 on cards & > AED 2000 on installment finances to be checked as per below:
    • For all schemes excluding Liability settlement
    • Expat: If AECB score is < 541 for all internal risk ratings or AECB score is ≥ 541 for Medium/High Risk
    • Local: If AECB score is < 541 for all internal risk ratings or AECB score is ≥ 541 for High Risk
    • For Liability settlement only for Very Low, Low or Medium Risk for both Expat and Local
    • Any overdue amount up to 29 DPD allowed for the facilities which are being settled, overdue on a facility that customer is not settling with LS must be settled by customer and should reflect as paid in AECB. AECB proof of overdue clearance is mandatory wherever applicable
    • For Liability settlement only for High Risk for both Expat and Local – If AECB score is ≥ 541
  3. AECB Worse Status will be checked only for High customers based on internal score if AECB score <541
  4. AECB score < 541 for Local under PDC segment will be referred
  5. For UAE nationals, overdue amounts under the Government Housing Programs may be disregarded up to Bucket 3 (i.e., < 90 DPD). Overdue in Bucket 4 and beyond must be settled and updated in AECB prior to approval. Applicable for only Liability segment and salary transfer segment
  6. For the non-scored segments, please refer to AECB policy for the applicable AECB parameters
IN SIMPLE WORDS
  • Instant rejection: AECB score exactly 300 (anyone, any segment); PDC with score below 541; Expat with AECB overdue above AED 10,000.
  • Current-overdue check (card overdue > AED 1,000 or instalment overdue > AED 2,000) is applied in these situations:
    • Expat: score < 541 (any risk) or score ≥ 541 with Medium/High risk.
    • Local: score < 541 (any risk) or score ≥ 541 with High risk.
    • Liability Settlement: Very Low/Low/Medium risk; and High risk if score ≥ 541.
  • In LS, facilities being settled may be overdue up to 29 DPD. Any overdue on a facility not being settled must be paid by the customer and show as paid in AECB (proof required).
  • "Worse status" in AECB is checked only for High-risk customers with score < 541.
  • Note the difference: rule I says PDC score < 541 → auto reject; rule IV says Local under PDC with score < 541 → referred.
  • UAE Nationals: overdue on Government Housing Program loans can be ignored up to Bucket 3 (< 90 DPD) – only for Liability segment and salary transfer segment. Bucket 4+ must be settled and updated in AECB before approval.
TRAINER NOTE
(1) Rule I (Expats and UAE Nationals, PDC, score < 541 → auto reject) and rule IV (Local, PDC, score < 541 → referred) appear to conflict for UAE Nationals. Confirm the system behaviour with RCPPM before training on this point.
(2) Rule II says the overdue amounts are "to be checked" but does not state the outcome (reject or refer). Follow the system/RCD instruction.

6.4 Debt Burden Ratio (DBR) CBUAE

POLICY TEXTPolicy page 6
  1. The DBR is calculated individually for each income type which is 30% is applied to the pension income, and 50% is applied to the salary income
  2. The DBR calculation for DIB staff is managed exclusively by DIB-HR. This ensures that staff DBR assessments are handled according to internal policies and process.
  3. When a customer has dual income streams (pension and salary), calculating DBR as 30% of pension and 50% of salary can be challenging. To address this, the Finnone system is designed to use 60% of the pension and 100% of the salary when calculating DBR. This approach ensures the result remains consistent with DBR calculations, and there will not be any breach of the DBR limits
  4. DBR calculation is not applicable for finance against TD/cash secured
IN SIMPLE WORDS
DBR = total monthly debt payments ÷ monthly income. All monthly obligations (existing + new PF instalment) must not exceed 50% of salary or 30% of pension. For DIB staff, HR calculates DBR. For cash-secured PF, no DBR is needed.
WORKED EXAMPLE 1 – SALARY ONLY
Salary = AED 20,000 Max obligations (50%) = AED 10,000 Existing obligations (AECB) = AED 3,000 Max new PF instalment = 10,000 − 3,000 = AED 7,000 per month
WORKED EXAMPLE 2 – PENSION + SALARY (WHY FINNONE USES 60% / 100%)
Pension AED 10,000, Salary AED 15,000 Method in point 1: 30% × 10,000 + 50% × 15,000 = 3,000 + 7,500 = AED 10,500 Finnone method: (60% × 10,000 + 100% × 15,000) × 50% = (6,000 + 15,000) × 50% = 21,000 × 50% = AED 10,500 ✔ same result
Taking 60% of pension and applying the 50% salary limit is the same as applying 30% to the pension – that is why there is no breach.

6.8 Regulatory Restrictions CBUAE

POLICY TEXTPolicy page 7 (numbering as printed)
  1. 1. In case of salary reduction for reasons other than retirement, the bank/company could adjust the DBR to 50% of the reduced salary by extending tenor (to be reported as arrangement in AECB), as per Article 20-3 of Notice No. 2901/2011 dated 28/4/2011, and in both cases, no bank or finance company should provide additional facilities/funds when the remaining tenor of personal finance exceeds the respective regulatory limits.
  2. 2. Personal finance should not be structured as an overdraft (OD).
  3. 3. 2. Bank's appointed External Auditors and their employees are restricted for retail products
    Any of the below external facilitates observed in AECB not allowed for further financing:
    1. National default debt settlement fund (NDDS)
    2. National loan Scheme (NLS) – however if NLS is being closed with a new finance within CB regulation of tenure, multiple, DSR then it is allowed. Please refer to Long Tenure Finance under annexure 1, Documentation and General Guidelines
  4. 2. When customer has a facility with remaining tenure more than prescribed by CBUAE for Personal Finance or Home Finance. Fresh finance can be extended to customers having government backed housing program's facility with tenure more than 300 months, barring customers who have ever been part of CBUAE NDDSF list
IN SIMPLE WORDS
  • Salary reduced (not retirement): DBR can be brought back to 50% of the new salary by extending the tenor; it is reported as an "arrangement" in AECB. While the remaining tenor is beyond regulatory limits, no new money can be given.
  • PF can never be an overdraft.
  • DIB's external auditors and their employees cannot get retail products.
  • No further financing if AECB shows NDDS (National Default Debt Settlement fund) or NLS (National Loan Scheme) – except if the NLS loan is being closed by a new finance within CB rules.
  • No further financing if the customer has a facility whose remaining tenure is longer than CBUAE allows for PF or HF – except government-backed housing program facilities with tenure above 300 months (unless the customer was ever on the CBUAE NDDSF list).
WORKED EXAMPLE
Customer U (UAE National) has a Sheikh Zayed Housing Program facility of 360 months and has never been on the NDDSF list → fresh PF can be extended.
Customer V's AECB shows an active NDDS facility → not allowed for further financing.

6.9 Affordability Assessment – Consumer Protection Regulation (CPR) CBUAE

POLICY TEXTPolicy page 7
The Consumer Protection Standards as set by the Central Bank of the UAE (CBUAE) in December 2020 is an essential pillar for strengthening consumer confidence by establishing transparency and promoting customer's interest. The Consumer Protection Regulation (CPR) emphasizes on disclosure, transparency and responsible business conduct through any distribution channels with special attention to the criteria which are part of Consumer Protection Standards.

To ensure compliance to CPR regulation, it is mandatory that the Bank assess its products suitability and affordability by assessing lifestyle expenditures of a customer. Please refer to Retail Credit Policy Document, Consumer Protection Standards for more details.

The affordability assessment is applicable to all salaried individuals and Self-Employed customers having company with legal status as Sole Establishment
IN SIMPLE WORDS
Passing DBR is not enough – the bank must also check that the customer can really afford the instalment after normal lifestyle expenses. This applies to all salaried customers and to self-employed customers whose company is a Sole Establishment.

6.10 PF not to be used for Home Finance down payment CBUAE

POLICY TEXTPolicy page 7
The level of down payment required from the home finance applicant should be from applicant's own resources and not from other source of finance including personal finance or covered cards (credit card for conventional banks). All such home finance applicants will not be allowed Personal Finance for a period of 2 months to ensure that proceeds are not used for Home Finance down payment ( (CBUAE Regulation 31/2013)
WORKED EXAMPLE
Customer W applied for Home Finance on 1 March. A PF application from the same customer on 15 April → not allowed (within 2 months). From 1 May onwards → can be considered.
