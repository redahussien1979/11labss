## Section A — Vocabulary: Word Meanings (Questions 1–20)

**1. Which word means "everything above the Earth"?** `[Book]`

- A. gravity
- B. space
- C. climate
- D. crew

> **Correct answer: B** — *Space* is defined as everything above (beyond) the Earth.

**2. An object that is sent into space to collect information is called a:** `[Book]`

- A. capsule
- B. rocket
- C. satellite
- D. launch pad

> **Correct answer: C** — A *satellite* is an object sent into space to collect information.

**3. The adjective "lunar" describes something connected with:** `[Book]`

- A. the Sun
- B. the Moon
- C. the stars
- D. the Earth

> **Correct answer: B** — *Lunar* means "of the Moon", so a *lunar capsule* is a Moon capsule.

**4. Someone whose job is to explore space is an:** `[Book]`

- A. astronomer
- B. architect
- C. athlete
- D. astronaut

> **Correct answer: D** — An *astronaut* is a person who travels to and explores space.

**5. The verb "to launch" means:** `[Book]`

- A. to send something up into the air
- B. to bring something down to the ground
- C. to repair a machine
- D. to travel slowly in a circle

> **Correct answer: A** — *Launch* = to send something into the air, as with a space rocket.

**6. The verb "to land" means:** `[Book]`

- A. to send something into the air
- B. to bring something down to the ground
- C. to grow food in space
- D. to float without moving

> **Correct answer: B** — *Land* = to bring something down onto a surface, e.g. "The lunar capsule lands on the Moon."

**7. A small container in which astronauts live during a space flight is a:** `[Book]`

- A. capsule
- B. station
- C. pad
- D. disc

> **Correct answer: A** — A *capsule* is a small container where astronauts live and travel.

**8. "Exploration" is:** `[Book]`

- A. the activity of going somewhere never visited by humans before
- B. the activity of staying safely in one place
- C. the job of repairing spacecraft
- D. the science of growing plants

> **Correct answer: A** — *Exploration* is the activity of travelling to a place that has never been visited.

**9. One inch is equal to:** `[Book]`

- A. 1.54 centimetres
- B. 2.54 centimetres
- C. 25.4 centimetres
- D. 39.37 centimetres

> **Correct answer: B** — An *inch* is 2.54 centimetres.

**10. A short audio or video file is called a:** `[Book]`

- A. disc
- B. record
- C. clip
- D. report

> **Correct answer: C** — A *clip* is a short audio or video file.

**11. "Research" means:** `[Book]`

- A. to look for information
- B. to travel very quickly
- C. to agree with another person
- D. to build a machine

> **Correct answer: A** — *Research* is looking carefully for information.

**12. Bad weather with rain, thunder and lightning is called a:** `[Book]`

- A. dust storm
- B. thunderstorm
- C. snowstorm
- D. sunrise

> **Correct answer: B** — A *thunderstorm* is bad weather with rain, thunder and lightning.

**13. A round, flat item such as a CD, a DVD or a record is a:** `[Book]`

- A. clip
- B. seed
- C. pad
- D. disc

> **Correct answer: D** — A *disc* is a round, flat item like a CD, DVD or record.

**14. The adjective "golden" means:** `[Book]`

- A. very old and broken
- B. made of gold, or gold-coloured
- C. extremely fast
- D. very heavy

> **Correct answer: B** — *Golden* = made of gold or gold in colour, as in the *Golden Record*.

**15. A person who works in science is a:** `[Book]`

- A. journalist
- B. presenter
- C. scientist
- D. passenger

> **Correct answer: C** — A *scientist* is a person who works in science.

**16. "To imagine" means:** `[Book]`

- A. to prove something with facts
- B. to think or dream about something
- C. to measure something carefully
- D. to build something with your hands

> **Correct answer: B** — *To imagine* is to think or dream about something that is not real.

**17. A type of book or film about space and the future is called:** `[Book]`

- A. science fiction
- B. a biography
- C. a documentary
- D. a newspaper

> **Correct answer: A** — *Science fiction* is a type of book or movie about science, space and the future.

**18. "To be proud" means to feel:** `[Book]`

- A. worried about a difficult problem
- B. tired after a long journey
- C. happy because you, or someone you know, did something good
- D. angry with a friend

> **Correct answer: C** — *To be proud* = to feel happy because you or someone you know did something good.

**19. Which word is the OPPOSITE of "launch"?** `[★ Extra]`

- A. explore
- B. land
- C. float
- D. imagine

> **Correct answer: B** — *Launch* sends something up into the air; *land* brings it back down. They are opposite actions.

**20. Which word in this list is a VERB?** `[★ Extra]`

- A. exploration
- B. lunar
- C. golden
- D. launch

> **Correct answer: D** — *Launch* is a verb (an action). *Exploration* is a noun; *lunar* and *golden* are adjectives.

## Section B — Vocabulary: Compound Nouns and Word Building (Questions 21–32)

**21. A compound noun is:** `[Book]`

- A. two words put together to make a new word
- B. a verb written in the past simple
- C. a noun that has no plural form
- D. a question word used with "if"

> **Correct answer: A** — A compound noun is made of two words joined together to make one new word, e.g. *launch + pad = launch pad*.

**22. A flat surface from which rockets are sent into space is a:** `[Book]`

- A. landing strip
- B. launch pad
- C. control room
- D. space capsule

> **Correct answer: B** — *Launch* (to send into the air) + *pad* (a flat surface) = *launch pad*.

**23. "Lunar landing" means:** `[Book]`

- A. to land a capsule on the Moon
- B. to launch a rocket from Earth
- C. to fly around the Sun
- D. to leave the space station

> **Correct answer: A** — *Lunar* (of the Moon) + *landing* = landing a capsule on the Moon.

**24. The air that astronauts breathe inside the capsule is the:** `[Book]`

- A. sunlight
- B. spaceflight
- C. air supply
- D. growing condition

> **Correct answer: C** — *Air supply* is the air that astronauts breathe inside the capsule.

**25. The light that comes from the sun is called:** `[Book]`

- A. moonlight
- B. sunlight
- C. artificial light
- D. starlight

> **Correct answer: B** — *Sun* + *light* = *sunlight*, the light from the sun.

**26. "Spaceflight" means:** `[Book]`

- A. to fly through space
- B. to walk on the Moon
- C. to build a rocket
- D. to watch the stars from Earth

> **Correct answer: A** — *Space* + *flight* = flying through space.

**27. "Upward" means:** `[Book]`

- A. to move down
- B. to move backwards
- C. to move up
- D. to stay still

> **Correct answer: C** — *Up* + *ward* = moving in an upward direction.

**28. A place where astronauts live in space and carry out research is a:** `[Book]`

- A. launch pad
- B. rocket engine
- C. space station
- D. space capsule

> **Correct answer: C** — A *space station*, such as the International Space Station, is where astronauts live and do research.

**29. When there is no force holding you down to the Earth, this is called:** `[Book]`

- A. zero gravity
- B. artificial light
- C. outer space
- D. extreme weather

> **Correct answer: A** — *Zero gravity* means there is no force pulling you towards the Earth, so astronauts float.

**30. Light that does NOT come from the sun is called:** `[Book]`

- A. daylight
- B. sunlight
- C. moonlight
- D. artificial light

> **Correct answer: D** — *Artificial light* is light made by people, for example from a lamp or a battery.

**31. Which of the following is NOT a compound noun?** `[★ Extra]`

- A. spacesuit
- B. sunlight
- C. astronaut
- D. launch pad

> **Correct answer: C** — *Astronaut* is a single word, not two words joined together. The others are all made of two words.

**32. The part of a rocket that burns fuel and produces power is the rocket:** `[★ Extra]`

- A. capsule
- B. engine
- C. pad
- D. satellite

> **Correct answer: B** — *Rocket* + *engine* = *rocket engine*, the part that gives the rocket its power.

- A. launch pad
- B. spacesuit

**34. The doctor checked the ______ of the sick patients before the flight.** `[Book]`

- A. climate

**35. Earth, Venus and Mars are all types of ______.** `[Book]`

**36. ______ is the measure of how warm or cold something is.** `[Book]`

**37. Many scientists say that ______ change is an important issue on our planet.** `[Book]`

**38. If you don't like the rainy ______, you shouldn't go out this evening.** `[Book]`

- B. climate

**39. The ______ on the two planets is very different: a person who weighs 100 lbs on Earth would weigh only 38 lbs on Mars.** `[Book]`

- A. temperature

**40. Mars has ______ weather conditions, with very strong winds and huge dust storms.** `[Book]`

## Section D — Grammar: The Second Conditional (Questions 45–62)

**45. We use the second conditional to talk about:** `[Book]`

- A. something that always happens, like a scientific fact
- B. something we imagine, which is not real now
- C. something that happened and finished in the past
- D. an instruction or an order

> **Correct answer: B** — The second conditional talks about something we imagine in the present or the future, but which is not real.

**46. Which is the correct structure of the second conditional?** `[Book]`

- A. If + present simple, will + infinitive
- B. If + will, would + -ing
- C. If + past simple, would + bare infinitive
- D. If + would, past simple

> **Correct answer: C** — The pattern is: **If + past simple, would + bare infinitive**, e.g. *If I went to space, I would take a camera.*

**47. If I ______ to space, I would take my camera.** `[Book]`

- A. go
- B. will go
- C. would go
- D. went

> **Correct answer: D** — After *if* in the second conditional we use the past simple: *went*.

**48. If Omar had a plane, he ______ to many countries.** `[Book]`

- A. will travel
- B. would travel
- C. travels
- D. travelled

> **Correct answer: B** — The result clause uses *would* + bare infinitive: *would travel*.

**49. If I ______ an astronaut, I would go to Mars.** `[★ Extra]`

- A. am
- B. were
- C. will be
- D. would be

> **Correct answer: B** — In the second conditional we use the past form of *be*, and *were* is used for all persons: *If I were an astronaut …*

**50. She would grow vegetables if she ______ on the space station.** `[★ Extra]`

- A. lives
- B. will live
- C. lived
- D. would live

> **Correct answer: C** — The *if* clause takes the past simple, even when it comes second in the sentence: *if she lived*.

**51. Which sentence is grammatically CORRECT?** `[★ Extra]`

- A. If I would have a rocket, I would fly to the Moon.
- B. If I had a rocket, I will fly to the Moon.
- C. If I had a rocket, I would fly to the Moon.
- D. If I have a rocket, I would flew to the Moon.

> **Correct answer: C** — Only C follows the rule *If + past simple, would + bare infinitive*. We never use *would* directly after *if* in this structure.

**52. Complete the negative sentence: "I ______ go to the sun — it is far too hot."** `[Book]`

- A. wouldn't
- B. didn't would
- C. not would
- D. won't would

> **Correct answer: A** — A negative second conditional result uses *wouldn't* + bare infinitive.

**53. If we ______ enough water, the corn wouldn't grow well.** `[★ Extra]`

- A. don't have
- B. didn't have
- C. wouldn't have
- D. haven't

> **Correct answer: B** — The *if* clause needs a negative past simple: *didn't have*.

**54. ______ you go to Mars if you had the chance?** `[Book]`

- A. Will
- B. Do
- C. Did
- D. Would

> **Correct answer: D** — To make a second conditional question we start with *Would* + subject + bare infinitive.

**55. Where ______ you go if you had a rocket?** `[Book]`

- A. will
- B. would
- C. do
- D. did

> **Correct answer: B** — After the question word we use *would*: *Where would you go …?*

**56. In the sentence "If I made a space time capsule, I'd include photos of Burj Khalifa", the short form "I'd" means:** `[Book]`

- A. I had
- B. I did
- C. I would
- D. I could

> **Correct answer: C** — In the second conditional, *'d* is the short form of *would*.

**57. If NASA sent out more Voyagers, they ______ many years to travel to the edge of the solar system.** `[Book]`

- A. will take
- B. take
- C. would take
- D. took

> **Correct answer: C** — The result clause of a second conditional uses *would* + bare infinitive: *would take*.

**58. If I ______ one million dirhams, I would take all my friends and family on a nice vacation.** `[Book]`

- A. have
- B. will have
- C. would have
- D. had

> **Correct answer: D** — The *if* clause uses the past simple: *had*.

**59. Look at these two sentences:
(1) "If I go to space, I will be happy."  (2) "If I went to space, I would be happy."
Which answer describes them correctly?** `[Book]`

- A. (1) is the first conditional and (2) is the second conditional.
- B. (1) is the second conditional and (2) is the first conditional.
- C. Both are second conditional sentences.
- D. Both are first conditional sentences.

> **Correct answer: A** — Sentence (1) uses *present simple + will* (first conditional, a real possibility). Sentence (2) uses *past simple + would* (second conditional, imagined).

**60. Which sentence talks about something the speaker only IMAGINES (it is not real)?** `[★ Extra]`

- A. If I have time, I will write another book next year.
- B. If I had a spaceship, I would visit Mars every weekend.
- C. When I go to space, I will take a camera with me.
- D. I visited the space museum last week.

> **Correct answer: B** — Only B uses the second conditional (*had … would visit*), which shows an imaginary situation.

**61. Put the words in the correct order:
"would / I / if / grow / went / vegetables / to space / I"** `[Book]`

- A. If I would go to space, I grew vegetables.
- B. I went to space if I would grow vegetables.
- C. If I went to space, I would grow vegetables.
- D. Would I go to space if I grew vegetables.

> **Correct answer: C** — The correct order follows *If + past simple, would + bare infinitive*.

**62. Complete the sentence from an interview about space: "Sports ______ be difficult in space, because the gravity is different."** `[Book]`

- A. will
- B. would
- C. are
- D. did

> **Correct answer: B** — The speaker is imagining life in space, so the second conditional form *would be* is used.

## Section E — Grammar: Noun Phrases and Functional Language (Questions 63–70)

**63. Which phrase would you use to AGREE with someone?** `[Book]`

- A. I'm not so sure.
- B. You're right.
- C. I don't really agree.
- D. I'm afraid that's wrong.

> **Correct answer: B** — *You're right*, *Me too* and *I agree* are used for agreeing.

**64. Which phrase would you use to DISAGREE politely with someone?** `[Book]`

- A. Me too.
- B. I agree.
- C. You're right.
- D. I'm not so sure.

> **Correct answer: D** — *I'm not so sure* and *I don't really agree* are used to disagree politely.

**65. In a compound noun, the first word usually:** `[Book]`

- A. describes the second word
- B. means the opposite of the second word
- C. replaces the second word
- D. changes the tense of the second word

> **Correct answer: A** — In a compound noun the first word describes the second, e.g. *space mission* tells you the exact type of mission.

**66. We use an expanded noun phrase in our writing in order to:** `[Book]`

- A. give the reader more information about the noun
- B. ask the reader a question
- C. show when the action happened
- D. make the sentence shorter

> **Correct answer: A** — Expanded noun phrases add extra detail so the reader knows more about what is being described.

**67. Which of these is an expanded noun phrase?** `[Book]`

- A. the planet
- B. she travelled
- C. the red planet in space
- D. very quickly

> **Correct answer: C** — *The red planet in space* is a noun phrase with extra information added (*red*, *in space*).

**68. In the noun phrase "the old spaceship", which word gives the additional detail?** `[Book]`

- A. the
- B. old
- C. spaceship
- D. the spaceship

> **Correct answer: B** — *Spaceship* is the main noun; *old* is the additional information that describes it.

**69. In the noun phrase "a tiny, rocky planet", the MAIN NOUN is:** `[★ Extra]`

- A. tiny
- B. rocky
- C. planet
- D. a

> **Correct answer: C** — *Planet* is the main noun; *tiny* and *rocky* are adjectives that describe it.

**70. Which sentence correctly expands the noun phrase "a beautiful sunset"?** `[★ Extra]`

- A. a beautiful sunset over the red mountains
- B. sunset beautiful a
- C. beautifully sunset
- D. the sunset beautiful

> **Correct answer: A** — A is a correct, natural expansion: the extra phrase *over the red mountains* adds more detail about the sunset.

## Section F — Reading Comprehension: Facts from the Unit Texts (Questions 71–80)

**71. In which year did scientists send the Golden Record into space?** `[Book]`

- A. 1957
- B. 1969
- C. 1977
- D. 2021

> **Correct answer: C** — The Golden Record was sent into space in **1977**.

**72. The Golden Record was carried into space on the spacecraft called:** `[Book]`

- A. Voyager 1
- B. Explorer One Dubai
- C. Apollo 11
- D. Sputnik

> **Correct answer: A** — NASA sent the Golden Record to space on **Voyager 1**.

**73. The Golden Record contains greetings in how many languages?** `[Book]`

- A. 12
- B. 40
- C. 50
- D. 55

> **Correct answer: D** — There are greetings in **55 languages** on the Golden Record.

**74. Which of these sounds from Earth can be found on the Golden Record?** `[★ Extra]`

- A. wind, rain and thunderstorms
- B. cars and aeroplanes
- C. telephones ringing
- D. football matches

> **Correct answer: A** — The record holds sounds from Earth such as **wind, rain and thunderstorms**, and even animal noises.

**75. Abdulla Al Shamsi, who wrote a science-fiction book about living on Mars, is a famous:** `[Book]`

- A. scientist
- B. athlete
- C. doctor
- D. journalist

> **Correct answer: B** — He is described as a famous **athlete** who has written a science-fiction book about living on Mars.

**76. According to the interview, why must astronauts who travel to Mars be strong?** `[Book]`

- A. Because they might live on Mars for a very long time and cannot just come back.
- B. Because they have to carry very heavy equipment on Earth.
- C. Because they are not allowed to sleep during the journey.
- D. Because they must build the rocket themselves.

> **Correct answer: A** — The astronauts have to be strong because they might be living on Mars for a very long time; they can't just come back.

**77. What is the astronaut Mona's mission at the International Space Station?** `[Book]`

- A. To repair a broken satellite.
- B. To take photographs of the surface of Mars.
- C. To help conduct science experiments to grow vegetables in space.
- D. To build a new launch pad.

> **Correct answer: C** — Her mission is to help conduct science experiments to grow vegetables, such as zucchini, in space.

**78. How long did the journey to the International Space Station take?** `[★ Extra]`

- A. Two hours
- B. Two days
- C. Two weeks
- D. Two months

> **Correct answer: B** — It is described as a **two-day trip** to the ISS, flying at over 28,000 kilometres per hour.

**79. In Mona's experiment, which plant grew the best?** `[Book]`

- A. The corn
- B. The zucchini
- C. The potatoes
- D. All three grew at exactly the same speed

> **Correct answer: C** — The **potatoes** grew the best — in fact, at the same rate as they would grow in nature.

**80. Why was the corn plant growing slowly?** `[Book]`

- A. It was getting enough sunlight, but not enough water.
- B. It was getting enough water, but not enough sunlight.
- C. It was getting no light at all.
- D. It was planted too late in the mission.

> **Correct answer: A** — The corn was getting just enough sunlight to grow, but not enough water to grow at a normal rate.
